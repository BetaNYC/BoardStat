BoardStat 2.0 (prototype)

See what New Yorkers report to 311 in your community district, your council district, a whole borough, or at one address: the most common request types, how long the city takes to close them, and where they happen.

Step 1: What do you want to look at?

A borough page adds up all of the borough's community districts and its parks and other Joint Interest Areas. Its charts open quickly; its map, list of requests and top addresses load only when you ask for them, because they need much more data.

Council districts use the current lines (Department of City Planning release 26c). Requests made before the 2023 redistricting are re-mapped to today's lines by their location, so totals may differ from the city's own council_district field.

Step 2: Pick your community district

Or select a district on the map below.

Step 2: Type an address

Select an address to see every request there over time, and how it compares with its community district and council district. This is not a geocoder: it only finds addresses that already appear in 311 requests in this data. Common short forms work (114 Nassau Ave, Brooklyn, NY 11222), but write numbered streets with numbers (5 Ave, not Fifth Ave). Press Enter to open the first match.

    Or click the map

    The map shows district boundaries only.

    About

    Data comes from NYC Open Data's 311 service requests and the Department of City Planning's district boundaries. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent anywhere.
